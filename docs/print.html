Market-making rules of thumb

  1. Quote around fair, width ∝ uncertainty. Tighten as info arrives.
  2. Skew against inventory. Long → lower bid AND ask.
  3. Getting filled is information (adverse selection). Repeated lifts = you're too low.
  4. Size small when unsure, bigger when edge is clear (fractional Kelly).
  5. Spread × volume is the business; don't turn into a directional gambler.
  6. Linked products must be consistent — check every one.
  7. Know your position at all times.

Common traps

  • Quoting a market your partner already crossed
  • Forgetting fees / contract multipliers
  • Settlement defined differently than you assumed (read it!)
  • Drawing cards without replacement — use card_draw_dist, not dice math
  • Chasing news that's already priced in
  • Panic-flattening at bad prices in the last minute

4. No-API formats

4a. Verbal / "make me a market" / estimation

  1. Price it: mkt dice 3, mkt cards 2 (after seen ...), mkt fermi 1k 100k, or mkt FAIR STD.
  2. Open wide (first line), tighten as info arrives. Say it with size: "9 at 12, 10 up".
  3. Someone keeps lifting you → gm X <price> moves your fair toward them. Repeated one-way flow = you're wrong.
  4. Reveals: seen K 7 then mkt cards N again. Dice reveals: reveal N_UNKNOWN v1 v2.
  5. Log every fill (b X 10 34), check p after each trade, never exceed limits.

4b. GUI exchange, no API

  1. Trader clicks; Analyst logs fills in live.py and watches fairs and p.
  2. Every few minutes: compare p with the official position screen. Fix with b/s/undo.
  3. News → news X impact% p_true priced_in, then pick off stale quotes first, then requote.

6. Fundamental Analyst Challenge

  1. Skim the packet for: price, shares, net debt, last revenue, growth/margin guidance, peers' multiples, EPS, 52-week range.
  2. Fill INPUTS in valuation.py, run python3 valuation.py (prints DCF, WACC×g grid, comps, reverse DCF, football field, pitch outline).
  3. Sanity checks before you speak:
  • Terminal value > 80% of EV → say your answer hinges on g/WACC and show the grid.
  • DCF vs comps disagree by > 30% → explain why (growth, margins, leverage) or adjust inputs.
  • Reverse DCF: "the price implies X% growth forever; we think that's too high/low because …" is a strong pitch line.
  1. Pitch structure (60–90 s): recommendation + target → valuation (3 methods) → what the market believes → 2–3 drivers → catalysts → risks + downside value.
  2. Likely judge questions — have one-sentence answers:
  • Why this WACC / beta? (CAPM build; peers' unlevered beta re-levered: py relever_beta(unlever_beta(b, de, t), de2, t))
  • What breaks the thesis? (name the input and its value in the grid where you'd flip)
  • Why will the market re-rate it, and when? (catalyst)
  • How would you size / hedge the position? (sector or peer short; position size as % of book)
  1. News during the round: py earnings_surprise_move(actual, consensus, price, pe=...) and news_fair_update(...).

7. Last 5 minutes (any game)

  1. Know the settlement rule cold. Recheck positions vs the official screen.
  2. Cancel stale orders. Reduce positions you can't defend at settlement.
  3. Don't start new complex trades. A small sure profit beats a large risky one now.

Playbooks by game type

A. Open-outcry "make me a market" (dice, cards, estimation)

  1. Fair = expectation, width ≈ 0.5–1 std early. Say it confidently: "34 at 38, 10 up."
  2. Each reveal → recompute (reveal, condition). Tighten width as std shrinks.
  3. If someone lifts your offer repeatedly, they think it's higher — shift up, don't keep selling.
  4. Inventory: if long, lower both quotes. Never let one position dominate.
  5. Estimation (Fermi) markets: geometric mean of your 90% bounds; quote very wide; trade on others' info.

B. Electronic exchange (GUI/API) with multiple products

  1. Market-make the liquid products with tight but skewed quotes.
  2. Run arb checks continuously (ETF vs basket, PCP). Execute both legs fast.
  3. Hit stale quotes after news. Cancel your own stale quotes FIRST.
  4. Near end: flatten if settlement is uncertain; hold if you know settlement value and price is wrong.

C. News / Fundamental Analyst round

  1. For each headline: direction, magnitude (% fair change), probability it's real, how much is priced in → news_fair_update.
  2. Trade the surprise vs expectations, not the headline.
  3. Be first or be skeptical: if price already moved fully, don't chase.
  4. Valuation questions: DCF/multiples in tt/fundamentals.py. Sanity check vs comparable multiples.

D. Options round

  1. Price with BS; get IV from market quotes. Compare IVs across strikes — outliers are trades.
  2. Check PCP, call-spread bounds, butterflies. Violations = free money.
  3. Delta-hedge large option positions with the underlying (delta_hedge_qty).

Risk rules (write them on paper)

  • Max loss per product before you stop and re-think: ____
  • Never exceed limits — breaches usually cost more than any trade earns
  • If confused about a product, quote wide or don't trade it
  • Recheck position vs official screen every ~10 min
